\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.} \emph{En$\rightarrow$Zh}: AMI~\citep{carletta2005ami} IHM-mix recordings (headsets summed into one
channel), official split; each utterance's translation is selected from three MT systems by reference-free quality
estimation and an audio check (Appendix~\ref{app:data}); test references are therefore machine translations. Reference
windows group overlapping or touching utterances (at most 20\,s): 2{,}375 test windows from 16 meetings, 35\% with
several speakers, keeping \num{v6:filt:en2zh:all:dur}\% of the test speech (Table~\ref{tab:funnel7}); whole-meeting runs
read all of it. \emph{Zh$\rightarrow$En}: each AMI utterance's Chinese translation is re-spoken by the UniSS Talker with
one voice per speaker at its original start; the human AMI transcripts are the references (Appendix~\ref{app:zhdata};
the same 16 test meetings), so this source is synthetic. \emph{Real Mandarin}: the AliMeeting test set~\citep{yu2022m2met}, 20
meetings of two to four people (10.8\,h), headset channels mixed into one (not the far-field condition of M2MeT or
DiariST); no AliMeeting data is used in training. AliMeeting is scored
against the human English translations released with DiariST~\citep{yang2024diarist}, except in the overlap study
(Table~\ref{tab:ovmain8}) and the appendix tables marked as such, which used machine translations of the human
transcripts (Appendices~\ref{app:qe7},~\ref{app:v9}).

\paragraph{Models.} Encoder and Thinker are Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct~\citep{xu2025qwen3omni}; the diarizer is
Streaming Sortformer~\citep{medennikov2025streaming} (offline and low-latency presets). \sys{} (offline) is one
bidirectional model (Table~\ref{tab:hyper}). The streaming \sys{} is one bidirectional model trained in two stages on 8
H200 GPUs (Appendix~\ref{app:v10train}). \emph{Stage 1}, the deadline baseline, trains the deadline objective from
\sys{} (offline) on \num{v10:c:j:rows} rows, every batch of 64 holding 32 rows per direction, for \num{v10:c:j:s1steps}
steps. \emph{Stage 2} trains write alignment from stage 1 with 64 read rows per step; checkpoints are decoded on five
development meetings per direction (dev5), and a rule fixed before stage 2 stops training and selects one. The rule
selected step \num{v10:c:j:s2rule}; we use step \num{v10:c:j:s2step}, which had higher dev5 BLEU but exceeds the rule's
latency bound (\S\ref{sec:res-hygiene}). The base Talker is a UniSS model~\citep{uniss2025} fine-tuned for block-wise
streaming speech; the PA Talker is fine-tuned from it per direction (Appendix~\ref{app:plem}). Decoding is greedy.

\paragraph{Systems.} \emph{B}: the stage-1 Thinker with the base Talker (fixed rate, one sample per block, no
merging). \emph{B+PA}: the same text, merged per read and spoken by the PA Talker. \emph{\sys{}}: the stage-2 Thinker
with $\bias=-1$ and the B+PA speech layer. Offline \emph{cascades} translate the diarizer's turns one by one with our
speaker-blind window model (\emph{same-data}), with the same recipe trained on the cascade's turns
(\emph{turn-trained}), or on reference turns (oracle input). SeamlessStreaming~\citep{seamless2023} is a zero-shot
external reference (Appendix~\ref{app:e7}).

\paragraph{Metrics.} \textbf{cpBLEU}, the translation analogue of concatenated minimum-permutation
WER~\citep{watanabe2020chime6}, scores windows: per meeting, output slots are matched one-to-one to reference speakers by
chrF~\citep{popovic2015chrf} and BLEU is computed over the matched pairs. Whole meetings are scored by \textbf{tcpBLEU},
the analogue of tcpWER~\citep{neumann2023meeteval}: each output segment joins the reference unit of its matched speaker
that it overlaps within a 2\,s collar (Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}); a unit translated again in a neighbouring window
keeps both translations, so duplication is not penalised (Appendix~\ref{app:dup}). BLEU is corpus-level
sacreBLEU~\citep{post2018call} (13a tokenisation for English, zh for Chinese); no learned metric (COMET, MetricX) is used.
\textbf{ASR-BLEU} is tcpBLEU of the speech transcribed by Whisper-large-v3~\citep{radford2023robust} (English) or
Paraformer~\citep{gao2022paraformer} (Chinese), averaged over two TTS seeds. \textbf{Latency} is measured per speaker
stream from audio and playback time, without computation. \emph{Text LAAL}~\citep{papi2022over} uses commit times.
Conventional speech LAAL charges every word of a block when the block starts playing (\emph{block-start}); our
\emph{LAAL$_\text{spread}$} spreads a block's words over its playback, so it also falls when blocks are shorter or
faster (Appendix~\ref{app:chunk}). We also report \emph{speech start} (first heard word of a unit) and \emph{EndOffset}
(unit end to the end of its last heard word).

\paragraph{Overlap-aware evaluation.}
\label{sec:eval-overlap}
A tcpBLEU unit is \emph{overlapped} when another speaker talks during at least 20\% of it, else \emph{single};
tcpBLEU-ov and tcpBLEU-1spk score each part. \emph{Concurrent-speaker recall} (CR) takes every pair of utterances of two
speakers that overlap by at least 0.5\,s and asks whether both received text attributed to their own speaker within
1\,s; \emph{collapse} counts pairs whose missing side is covered by the other side's speaker. \emph{Post-hoc relabelling}
gives each segment of a speaker-blind model the diarizer slot most active over its span, one speaker per span as in
recognition-then-attribution pipelines; online it uses only the frames available at the commit (\emph{causal}).

\paragraph{Statistics.} Confirmatory tests of the offline study were fixed in an internal analysis plan before
decoding (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}) and need Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ \emph{and} a 95\% interval, widened by decoder
noise, that excludes zero (Appendix~\ref{app:stats7}). Streaming systems (Table~\ref{tab:sig10}) use an exact sign-flip
test and a paired bootstrap over test meetings, pooling both TTS seeds, Holm-corrected over twelve tests, without the
widening. Latency differences are point estimates.
