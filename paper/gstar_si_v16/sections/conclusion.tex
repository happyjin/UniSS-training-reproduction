\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

When an interpretation is heard is largely decided after its text is written, by each speaker's speech queue.
\sys{} decodes speaker-first and trains its Talker on a latency-constrained playback-queue CRF; by the proposed
cpASR-BLEU and tcpASR-BLEU, it beats a same-text baseline in speech quality and latency on every test set,
including out-of-domain meetings, and leads all compared systems.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:res-hygiene}

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Evaluation is automatic: there is no listening test of voice, naturalness or
comprehensibility, and the En$\rightarrow$Zh references are machine translations without a bilingual check. The
Zh$\rightarrow$En AMI source is synthetic, and both AMI directions share 16 meetings. The test meetings were scored
repeatedly during development, so only the eight locked AliMeeting meetings form a blind test; AliMeeting is
out-of-domain for our models but not for the off-the-shelf diarizer. External systems run zero-shot on our windows,
with our diarizer's speaker labels.

\paragraph{Method.} The CRF ranks a few sampled renderings on a discretised backlog with fixed weights, and the Talker
is trained on its posterior in one round, along the base-rate backlog trajectory. Every model was trained once, with
one seed and one latency budget. Faster speech brings more pauses, which the potentials do not price.

\paragraph{System.} Committed output is never revised, so a wrong speaker label is spoken; a speaker who never talks
alone gets no voice prompt; and with one channel per speaker, overlapping translations are heard at once. Latencies
exclude computation, and as implemented, decoding does not keep up in real time (Appendix~\ref{app:online}).

\section*{Ethics Statement}

Speaker-attributed interpretation reproduces meeting participants' voices in another language. Voice cloning can be
misused for impersonation, so deployments should obtain participants' consent and mark synthetic speech. Our Chinese AMI
test audio clones AMI participants' voices. AMI (CC BY 4.0) was recorded with consent; AliMeeting (CC BY-SA 4.0) is used
for testing only.
