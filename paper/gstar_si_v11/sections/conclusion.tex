\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading the diarizer inside the decoder lets \sys{} attribute overlapping speech. Once translations are spoken in each
speaker's voice, much of the listener's delay is queueing of synthesised speech; aligning playback to this backlog
brings significant gains in speech ASR-BLEU and in both conventional and playback-aware speech latency, mostly by
speaking faster\ifFluencyPA{}, and a rate budget in the same posterior-regularised objective limits that cost\else{},
with more pauses\fi. Making the write time latent and training it on the decoder's likelihood does not beat deadlines:
the likelihood itself rewards waiting. With one channel per speaker, a listener hears several voices at once during
overlap.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Non-blind procedure.} The test meetings served throughout development (\S\ref{sec:res-hygiene})%
\ifAliEval{}; only the eight unused AliMeeting meetings give a locked test, and they cover Zh$\rightarrow$En only\fi.
The write-alignment rows additionally depend on a bias introduced after a first test run and on a checkpoint that
deviates from the selection rule (\S\ref{sec:write}).

\paragraph{Incomplete attribution of effects.}\ifFactorial{} The factorial ablation (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}) uses
one rate-matched rule; other rules could do better.\else{} We did not isolate the speech-layer components (merging, rate
rule, candidate selection, learned Talker) on the baseline text.\fi{} The Zh$\rightarrow$En PA Talker was trained on
outputs of a separately trained write-aligned Thinker, not on the deadline Thinker's text. Every streaming Thinker was
trained once, with one seed; the latency budget was run with one value. Fixed-lag, LocalAgreement, cascade and
relabelling baselines were run only with the per-direction model under an earlier look-ahead protocol
(Appendix~\ref{app:v9proto}), not with the final speech layer.

\paragraph{Computation.} Latencies count audio and playback time, not computation; the decoder re-encodes the prefix
at every read and the PA Talker samples two candidates per block.\ifWallclock{} Our wall-clock replay
(Appendix~\ref{app:wallclock}) covers few meetings on one hardware setting.\else{} We did not measure the cost of the
final system.\fi{} All streaming latencies assume one channel per speaker.

\paragraph{Latency and speech.} LAAL$_\text{spread}$ is not the conventional speech LAAL, and its word times are spread
over a block, not forced-aligned; LAAL may degenerate when most words are written at a window's last read
(Appendix~\ref{app:decomp}). The faster speech of the PA Talker comes with more pauses, including mid-sentence pauses,
which UTMOS does not capture; speaker similarity was measured against the Talker's voice prompt, not the speakers' own
recordings. Speech-latency tests are not corrected for multiple comparisons.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} No human evaluation: no listening test of voice identity, naturalness or comprehensibility,
and no bilingual check of the machine-translated En$\rightarrow$Zh references, which come from the same pipeline as the
training targets. tcpBLEU keeps duplicate translations of a unit and so partly rewards duplication
(Appendix~\ref{app:dup})\ifMetricsV{}; deduplicated tcpBLEU, COMET and a second recogniser are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:metricsv11}\fi. The Zh$\rightarrow$En AMI source is synthetic (re-voiced by the same model family as
our Talker), the two AMI directions share their 16 meetings, and on AliMeeting the text of write alignment does not
improve. AliMeeting audio is mixed close-talk channels and we do not report DiariST's own metric, so the numbers are not
comparable with published results; the reference windows keep \num{v6:filt:en2zh:all:dur}\% of the En$\rightarrow$Zh
test speech. Greedy bf16 decoding is not batch-invariant (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}).

\paragraph{System.} The diarizer, the encoder and the Thinker are trained separately from the Talker. Committed output
is never revised, so a wrong speaker label is spoken. Voice prompts come from the diarizer's solo regions, which a
speaker who never talks alone lacks.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

Speaker-attributed interpretation processes the voices of meeting participants and reproduces them in another
language. Voice cloning can be misused to impersonate people; a deployed system should obtain the participants'
consent and mark synthesised speech as such. Our Chinese AMI test audio clones the voices of AMI participants into
Chinese and is synthetic speech. AMI is distributed under a CC BY 4.0 licence and was recorded with the participants'
consent; AliMeeting (CC BY-SA 4.0) is used for testing only.
