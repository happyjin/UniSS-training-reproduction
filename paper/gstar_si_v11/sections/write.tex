\section{Is There a Better Time to Write?}
\label{sec:write}
\label{sec:method-latent}

Deadlines fix when each chunk is written. \emph{Write alignment} (WA) lets the decoder's own likelihood choose instead.
A write plan gives, for every read $r$, the set $A_r$ of a window's chunks written so far (each chunk on time or one
read late, each speaker's chunks in order, and whether newly due chunks precede deferred ones). Scoring each transition
by the Thinker's log-likelihood of the new tokens, given the \emph{canonical} serialisation of the chunks already
written, makes the tempered posterior $q_\tau$ over plans a linear-chain CRF with exact forward--backward
(Appendix~\ref{app:crf}). Each training step re-scores the lattice and minimises the posterior-weighted read loss
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss}), an EM-style self-training on a tempered posterior ($\tau=0.5$); stage 2 trains it from the
deadline Thinker for \num{v10:c:j:s2step} further steps.

\paragraph{The posterior drifts late.} One more read makes the same translation more likely and the lattice can only
defer, so the posterior mass on the deadline plan falls from \num{v10:drift:v2p:q_first} to
\num{v10:drift:v2p:q_last} during training (Table~\ref{tab:drift10}), and on development meetings the decoder writes
\num{v10:dev:z:m0-e2:tlaal} / \num{v10:dev:e:m0-e2:tlaal}\,s later (text LAAL), the effect that made MILk and MMA add
latency losses~\citep{arivazhagan2019milk,ma2020mma}. A training-time budget and a lattice that may also write early
do not undo it (Appendix~\ref{app:calib}); a constant bias $\bias=-1$ on the wait logit, set on five development
meetings, brings the lag back near the deadline model's.
