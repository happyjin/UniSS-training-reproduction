\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

When a listener hears an interpretation is largely decided after its text is written, by the queue of synthesised
speech in each speaker's stream. A linear-chain CRF over this queue, distilled into the speech generator, improves
speaker-attributed speech BLEU and latency over a deadline baseline with the same text on three test sets and on unused
meetings, most where queues are longest; the full system leads public and same-data multi-speaker speech-to-speech
systems on every set.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Non-blind procedure.}\label{sec:res-hygiene} All systems were run once on test and the PA Talker was chosen
on development data, but the test meetings served throughout development, so adaptive overfitting cannot be ruled out%
\ifAliEval{}; only the eight unused AliMeeting meetings give a locked test, and they cover Zh$\rightarrow$En only;
with eight meetings its sign-flip $p$-values cannot fall below $2/256$, and its absolute scores are low\fi.

\paragraph{External systems.} SeamlessStreaming and X-Translator run zero-shot on our detector windows and are
attributed by our diarizer, not as deployed by their authors; X-Translator's AMI input level was set by a constant gain
calibrated on dev in place of its browser's gain control, and both external systems have one TTS run against two for
ours.

\paragraph{The CRF and its training.} The candidate sets are eight samples of the base Talker, the backlog is
discretised, and the potential weights were fixed, not learned; the posterior is distilled in one round, on the backlog
trajectory under the base rate rather than marginalised over it. The PA Talkers were trained on text of separately
trained single-direction Thinkers, not on the deadline Thinker's text. Every model was trained once, with one
seed, and the latency budget was run with one value.

\paragraph{Computation.} Latencies count audio and playback time, not computation; the decoder re-encodes the prefix
at every read and the PA Talker samples two candidates per block.\ifWallclock{} \num{v11:wc:verdict}; the replay
covered few meetings on one hardware setting.\fi{} All streaming latencies assume one channel per speaker.

\paragraph{Latency and speech.} LAAL$_\text{spread}$ is not the conventional speech LAAL, and its word times are spread
over a block, not forced-aligned. The faster speech of the PA Talker comes with more pauses, including mid-sentence
pauses, which our potentials do not price and UTMOS does not capture; speaker similarity was measured against the
Talker's voice prompt, not the speakers' own recordings. Speech-latency tests are not corrected for multiple
comparisons.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} No human evaluation: no listening test of voice identity, naturalness or comprehensibility,
and no bilingual check of the machine-translated En$\rightarrow$Zh references, which come from the same pipeline as the
training targets. tcpBLEU keeps duplicate translations of a unit and so partly rewards duplication; it is the same for
all rows of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, which share their text. The Zh$\rightarrow$En AMI source is synthetic (re-voiced by
the same model family as our Talker), and the two AMI directions share their 16 meetings. AliMeeting audio is mixed
close-talk channels and we do not report DiariST's own metric, so the numbers are not comparable with published
results.

\paragraph{System.} The diarizer, the encoder and the Thinker are trained separately from the Talker. Committed output
is never revised, so a wrong speaker label is spoken. Voice prompts come from the diarizer's solo regions, which a
speaker who never talks alone lacks. With one output channel per speaker, a listener hears several voices at once
during overlap; how listeners cope with this is untested.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

Speaker-attributed interpretation processes the voices of meeting participants and reproduces them in another
language. Voice cloning can be misused to impersonate people; a deployed system should obtain the participants'
consent and mark synthesised speech as such. Our Chinese AMI test audio clones the voices of AMI participants into
Chinese and is synthetic speech. AMI is distributed under a CC BY 4.0 licence and was recorded with the participants'
consent; AliMeeting (CC BY-SA 4.0) is used for testing only.
