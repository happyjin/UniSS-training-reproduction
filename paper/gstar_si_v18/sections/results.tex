\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:res-sys}

Does \sys{} translate meetings better than existing multi-speaker speech-to-speech systems? It obtains the highest
cpASR-BLEU and tcpASR-BLEU on all three sets, including the out-of-domain AliMeeting meetings (Table~\ref{tab:main}),
although the others receive our diarizer's speaker labels. The public
systems keep only a third to three quarters of \sys{}'s tcpASR-BLEU, though partly heard earlier: SeamlessStreaming translates each window as one speaker and
X-Translator tracks speakers only to choose the cloned voice, so concurrent contributions are lost or misattributed and
their lower delay is bought with content (Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff}, Appendix~\ref{app:more}). The same-data cascade,
translating speaker-blind and attributing afterwards, reaches B's quality but is heard later, whereas \sys{} is better
and earlier than either on every set.

\subsection{Where the Listener-Side Delay Is Removed}
\label{sec:res-delay}

As \sys{} and B commit identical text, every difference arises in the speech streams. Table~\ref{tab:decomp} splits
EndOffset into the Thinker's lag, the detector's wait, the queue wait and the last block; the first two barely change.
Under B every stream produces more speech than its source holds (load above one) and queues as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:backlog}; the playback layer brings the load to about one, removes most of the queue and shortens the
last block, which account for the entire reduction. It is largest on AliMeeting, whose streams queue longest: the queue
depends only on speech durations and release times, so the playback layer carries over to out-of-domain meetings
without adaptation. The automatic MOS estimate rises and similarity to each voice prompt is unchanged.

\subsection{Out-of-Domain Generalisation and Robustness}
\label{sec:res-robust}

Do the gains over B hold with another recogniser and on meetings never decoded before? A second
recogniser, not the one used for selection, and COMET both prefer \sys{} on every set
(Table~\ref{tab:robust}). On eight AliMeeting meetings never decoded before, with systems and tests fixed in advance,
\sys{} again improves tcpASR-BLEU, LAAL and EndOffset, by margins at least as large as on the test set: the
playback layer, set on AMI only, carries over to another corpus of real Mandarin meetings. Our metrics
also agree with an independent one: on AliMeeting they rank \sys{}, B, X-Translator and SeamlessStreaming as DiariST's
official speaker-attributed BLEU does (Table~\ref{tab:diarist}).

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
\label{sec:res-ablation}
\label{sec:res-overlap}

\paragraph{Speaker tokens under overlap.} Would attributing a speaker-blind translation afterwards suffice? We train two simultaneous Thinkers per direction alike, with speaker tokens and with them zeroed,
and relabel the latter causally (Table~\ref{tab:overlap}). On both AMI sets, speaker tokens raise tcpBLEU on overlapped
units by about eight and a half points, more than on all units, and concurrent-speaker recall by 17--26 points: once a
speaker-blind model has merged two concurrent contributions, relabelling cannot split them, whereas speaker-first
decoding (Eq.~\eqref{eq:serial}) translates them as two segments (Figure~\ref{fig:motivation}). On the out-of-domain
AliMeeting meetings, recall rises by 17 points and overlapped units slightly (9.18 vs.\ 8.55), while all units are 1.2
points lower.

\paragraph{Playback-layer components.} Table~\ref{tab:ablation} adds the components to B one at a time, on the same
text. Read-level merging changes little; the backlog-aware rate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rate} gives the largest step in quality
and EndOffset, keeping each stream's load near one. Under the same rate rule, the PA Talker, trained on the CRF posterior, raises
tcpASR-BLEU on every set and ends speech 0.15--0.23\,s earlier (R3 against R2), most on AliMeeting, whose streams queue
longest: the backward messages of Eq.~\eqref{eq:w} reward shorter renderings where later blocks would otherwise wait. The budget set its operating point without tuning ($\lambda^\star=0.50$ / $0.70$). Choosing the more intelligible of two samples adds quality without changing EndOffset.
