\section{Data Construction and Statistics}
\label{app:data}

\paragraph{Reference windows.} Utterances that overlap or touch are grouped into reference windows of at most 20\,s
(Table~\ref{tab:data}); training targets, text references and the overlap study use these windows, whereas test
decoding runs on whole meetings through online detector windows (Appendix~\ref{app:online}).

\input{generated/tab_data}

\paragraph{Translations.} Every AMI utterance (IHM segmentation, official split) is translated by three MT systems
(Hy-MT2-30B-A3B, NiuTrans LMT-60-4B, MiLMMT-46-4B); one candidate is selected by reference-free quality estimation
(XCOMET-XL~\citep{guerreiro2024xcomet} and an XLM-R-XL estimator) and chrF agreement, synthesised in the speaker's
voice and checked for audibility and intelligibility. A window enters the data only if all its non-hesitation
utterances have a translation whose speech decodes.

\paragraph{Chinese AMI meetings.}\label{app:zhdata} Every utterance's Chinese translation is re-spoken by the UniSS
Talker with one voice prompt per speaker, trimmed, set to the speaker's median loudness in the English recording,
placed at the utterance's original start and time-stretched towards the English duration within $[0.8,1.5]$; a
speaker's next clip waits until the previous one ends. Clips failing an intelligibility check (CER $>0.3$) are left out
of the meeting, and 13 training meetings whose diarization error stayed high were dropped (none in test).

\paragraph{AliMeeting.} AliMeeting is used for testing only. The main results use DiariST's human English
translations~\citep{yang2024diarist}; the overlap study (Table~\ref{tab:overlap}) uses machine
translations (Hy-MT2-30B) of the human transcripts, without a human check. The eight AliMeeting evaluation meetings
(\num{v11:ae:hours}\,h) of the locked test were never used for training, development or any earlier test.

\subsection{Speaker tokens and the deadline Thinker}
\label{app:train-thinker}
\label{app:model}
\label{app:prompt}

\paragraph{Speaker tokens.} The diarizer's posteriors $\bm{P}\in[0,1]^{F\times S}$ (10\,ms outputs of Nemotron 3
Diarization mean-pooled to 80\,ms frames) are resampled to the acoustic token centres,
$\tilde{\bm{P}}=\mathrm{Interp}(\bm{P};\bar{t}_{1:N})$, zero-padded to $MK$ rows, and every block $\bm{P}_j$ of $K$ rows
becomes one speaker token,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bm{e}_j&=\textstyle\sum_{m=1}^{K}\bm{V}^{(m)}\tilde{\bm{p}}_{(j-1)K+m}+\bm{c},\\
\bm{s}_j&=\bm{V}_2\,\mathrm{GELU}\big(\bm{V}_1\mathrm{GELU}(\bm{e}_j)+\bm{c}_1\big)+\bm{c}_2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:conv}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\mathrm{Proj}_\psi$ is a strided convolution followed by a two-layer perceptron with weights $\bm{V}^{(m)},\bm{V}_1,\bm{V}_2$ and biases $\bm{c},\bm{c}_1,\bm{c}_2$; $\bm{V}_2,\bm{c}_2$ start at zero, so training starts from
the pretrained Thinker. Per meeting, reference speakers $\varrho$ are mapped to slots by the injective map $\sigma^\star$
that maximises the number of frames in which $\varrho$ speaks and slot $\sigma(\varrho)$ is active ($P>0.5$); an
utterance by $\varrho$ is labelled $\sigma^\star(\varrho)$. Hesitations have no target segment. The instruction asks for
\texttt{<|spk k|><|t a|>}translation\texttt{<|t b|>} per utterance, $k$ the slot, $a,b$ in 80\,ms steps, utterances in
order of start. During training the posteriors are perturbed (Table~\ref{tab:hyper}).

\paragraph{Deadlines.} An utterance's reference translation is split at punctuation into chunks $l=1,\dots,J$ of
$n_l$ target units; chunk $l$ covers the fraction $\varphi_l=\sum_{l'\le l}n_{l'}/\sum_{l'}n_{l'}$ of the utterance and
ends at the end of source word $\lceil\varphi_l|\bm{w}|\rceil$ of the time-aligned source words $\bm{w}$, or at
$a+\varphi_l(b-a)$ where no word timings exist (the re-voiced Chinese source). This end time is the $e_l$ of the
deadline rule in \S\ref{sec:method-thinker}.

\paragraph{Training.} The deadline Thinker is initialised from a Thinker trained on whole reference windows (the
offline model of Table~\ref{tab:hyper2}, adapters merged). The deadline rows of the two directions are interleaved into
\num{v10:c:j:rows} rows such that every global batch of 64 holds 32 Zh$\rightarrow$En and 32 En$\rightarrow$Zh rows;
there is no checkpoint selection, and the Thinker is the last step. Reads with nothing due are kept with probability
0.5; whole-window (20\%) and single-speaker (15\%) rows are mixed in.

\subsection{Talker, voice prompts and speaking rate}
\label{app:train-talker}

\paragraph{Voice prompts.} Slot $k$'s solo frames are
\begin{equation}
m_{f,k}=\mathds{1}\big[P_{f,k}>0.5\big]\textstyle\prod_{k'\neq k}\mathds{1}\big[P_{f,k'}<0.5\big].
\label{eq:alone}
\end{equation}
Runs of at least 0.4\,s of solo frames are concatenated into the voice prompt $\bm{v}_k$ (at most 8\,s), using only audio
heard before the slot's first commitment; the Talker encodes it into 32 BiCodec global tokens~\citep{wang2025spark} and
speaks each block as BiCodec semantic tokens, one per 20\,ms.

\paragraph{Speaking rate.} The Talker's rate token takes one of 35 values. B's base Talker uses one fixed value for
every block. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:rate}, the pace $\tau^{\mathrm{pace}}_i$ is the rate token whose natural duration for the
block's text matches the source time since the previous release; the backlog term is applied once the backlog reaches
0.5\,s, a sentence-final block is raised by at least two steps, and the result is clipped to the token range. Within a
stream, segment $n$ of duration $D_n$ then starts at
\begin{equation}
o_n=\max\big(\zeta_n,\;o_{n-1}+D_{n-1}\big),
\label{eq:layout}
\end{equation}
with $\zeta_n$ its release time; this is the queue of Eq.~\eqref{eq:backlog} before discretisation.

\paragraph{Candidates and fidelity.} For every block the base Talker samples 8 candidates: 4 at the base rate and 4
at two rate steps faster. $f(c)=-\min(\text{CTC loss per character},10)$ under an independent wav2vec2 CTC

brute-force enumeration on small streams.

\paragraph{Objective.} The Talker, conditioned on a backlog token (0.5\,s bins, capped at 3.5\,s), minimises

term covers the semantic codes and the end token, only candidates with $w_i(c)\ge$ \num{v10:c:plem:minq} are kept, and
all Talker parameters are trained.

\section{Inference and Online Processing}
\label{app:online}
\label{app:chunk}
\label{app:causal}

\paragraph{Online windows.} A test meeting is processed whole. A window opens at the first frame where any diarizer
slot is active and closes after 0.6\,s with no active slot or at 20\,s. Slots persist across windows; the decoder
restarts at each window, reading every $\Delta$ seconds as in \S\ref{sec:method-thinker}. All systems of
Table~\ref{tab:main} translate the same windows.

\paragraph{Chunk protocol.} The diarizer's 1.04\,s profile emits frames in blocks of nine; frame $f$ (80\,ms) is known
at $t^{\mathrm{rel}}_f=(9\lfloor f/9\rfloor+13)\cdot 80\,\text{ms}\le t_f+\gamma$, $\gamma=1.04$\,s. A window's opening is
released at $t^{\mathrm{rel}}$ of its first active frame and its end at $t^{\mathrm{rel}}$ of the last frame of the
closing gap; commits before the opening are delayed to it, and those of the last read to the release of the end. All
latencies are scored under this protocol.

\paragraph{Causality.} Simultaneous runs read the posteriors of one run of the diarizer's 1.04\,s profile over the
whole meeting, with every frame later than $t-\gamma$ zeroed at a read at time $t$. For three windows of every test
meeting we truncated the audio at a read time and re-ran the diarizer from scratch: over \num{v6:caus:cuts} cuts the
largest absolute posterior difference was \num{v6:caus:maxabs} (fp16 precision), and every differing $>0.5$ decision
was an fp16 rounding tie within $5\cdot10^{-4}$ of 0.5. The audio encoder encodes the heard prefix at every read.

\paragraph{Computation-aware analysis.} All latencies in the paper are computation-excluded: they count audio and
playback time only, as SimulEval's default latency does. Replayed with measured compute times on one GPU per system,
\sys{}'s Thinker needs 0.65 / 0.80 / 1.20\,s of computation per second of meeting (AMI Zh$\rightarrow$En /
En$\rightarrow$Zh / AliMeeting) and its PA Talker 0.18 / 0.26 / 0.31\,s. The Thinker re-encodes the heard prefix at every
read (batch-1 decoding without an incremental cache), so the system as implemented does not run in real time;
incremental encoding is left to future work. The replay covered few meetings on one hardware setting.

\section{Playback Alignment: Formal Definition and Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\input{figures/pa_lattice}

\paragraph{Formal definition.} Fix $\lambda$ and write $\phi_i=\phi^\lambda_i$. For a stream
$\mathcal{U}=(\bm{\nu},\bm{W},C_{1:I})$, position $i$ takes labels $z_i=(h_i,c_i)$ in the finite set $\mathcal{H}\times C_i$,
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:crf} factorises as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p_\lambda(\bm{z}\mid\mathcal{U})={}&\frac{\psi_0(z_1)}{Z_\lambda(\mathcal{U})}\prod_{i=1}^{I}\psi_i(z_i)\\
&\times\prod_{i=1}^{I-1}\psi_{i,i+1}(z_i,z_{i+1}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:crffactor}
\end{equation}
with node factors $\psi_i(z_i)=e^{\phi_i(z_i)}>0$, the start factor $\psi_0(z_1)=\mathds{1}[h_1=0]$ and pairwise
factors $\psi_{i,i+1}(z_i,z_{i+1})=\mathds{1}[h_{i+1}=g_i(h_i,c_i)]\in\{0,1\}$. This is a chain-structured random field
conditioned on $\mathcal{U}$ whose log-potentials are linear in the features $(\bar\ell(c),f(c),L_i(h,c))$ with weights
$(\eta_\ell,\eta_f,-\lambda)$ and whose pairwise log-potentials are $0$ or $-\infty$, i.e.\ a linear-chain
CRF~\citep{lafferty2001crf}. Its feature weights are fixed, not learnt: the CRF is used for inference only. The messages
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb} have entries
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_i(h)&=\textstyle\sum_{c_{<i}:\,h_i=h}\exp\sum_{j<i}\phi_j(z_j),\\
\beta_i(h)&=\textstyle\sum_{c_{\ge i}\mid h_i=h}\exp\sum_{j\ge i}\phi_j(z_j),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:msg}
\end{equation}
the total weight of the feasible prefixes that leave block $i$ with backlog $h$, and of all completions from there; a
feasible plan is determined by its candidates, since $c_{1:I}$ fixes every backlog from $h_1=0$. We write
$e(\bm{z})=\exp\sum_i\phi_i(z_i)$ for the unnormalised weight of a plan.

\paragraph{Modelling assumptions.} Inference is exact within the state space, which approximates the playback problem
in three ways. (i) $C_i$ holds a finite sample of renderings (Table~\ref{tab:hyper}), so the CRF ranks sampled
renderings, not all renderings. (ii) Backlogs are rounded up to the grid $\mathcal{H}$ and capped at $\bar H$, so $g_i$
approximates Eq.~\eqref{eq:backlog}; rounding up over-estimates the backlog, and on meeting-long chains it accumulated a
mean drift of 0.28\,s in a diagnostic run (our streams are per window and speaker, and shorter). (iii) The quality
features of a candidate do not depend on its backlog; only $L_i$ does.

\begin{proposition}[Exact inference]
\label{prop:fb}
For the CRF of Eq.~\eqref{eq:crf}, the messages of Eq.~\eqref{eq:msg} satisfy the recursions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb};
for every $i$, $Z_\lambda=\sum_h\alpha_i(h)\beta_i(h)$, in particular $Z_\lambda=\beta_1(0)$; and the marginals are the
entries of $\bm{\mu}_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fb}. The cost is $O(I\,|\mathcal{H}|\max_i|C_i|)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Factorisation.} The backlogs after block $i$ are computed from $h_i$ and $c_i,c_{i+1},\dots$ alone: $c_{<i}$
influences them only through $h_i$. Hence the weight of a plan splits at any block,
\[
e(\bm{z})=\Big(\textstyle\prod_{j<i}e^{\phi_j(h_j,c_j)}\Big)\Big(\textstyle\prod_{j\ge i}e^{\phi_j(h_j,c_j)}\Big),
\]
a prefix factor that ends at $h_i$ times a suffix factor that starts at $h_i$ and depends on the prefix only through
$h_i$.
\emph{Partition function.} Summing $e(\bm{z})$ over all feasible plans and grouping them by the value $h$ of $h_i$, the
prefix sums give $\alpha_i(h)$ and the suffix sums $\beta_i(h)$, so $Z_\lambda=\sum_h\alpha_i(h)\beta_i(h)$. At $i=1$,
$\alpha_1(h)=\mathds{1}[h=0]$ gives $Z_\lambda=\beta_1(0)$.
\emph{Recursions.} A prefix ending at $h_{i+1}=h'$ is a prefix ending at some $h_i=h$ followed by a candidate $c$ with
$g_i(h,c)=h'$; its weight is the old weight times $e^{\phi_i(h,c)}$, which is entry $h'$ of
$\sum_c\bm{T}_i^{(c)\top}(\bm{\alpha}_i\odot e^{\bm{\phi}_i^{(c)}})$. A suffix starting at $h_i=h$ is a candidate $c$ followed
by a suffix starting at $g_i(h,c)$, which is entry $h$ of $\sum_ce^{\bm{\phi}_i^{(c)}}\odot\bm{T}_i^{(c)}\bm{\beta}_{i+1}$.
\emph{Marginals.} The plans with $z_i=(h,c)$ are a prefix ending at $h$, the state $(h,c)$ and a suffix starting at
$g_i(h,c)$, with total weight $\alpha_i(h)e^{\phi_i(h,c)}\beta_{i+1}(g_i(h,c))$; dividing by $Z_\lambda$ gives
$\mu_i(h,c)$, and $\mathbb{E}_{p_\lambda}[\Phi]=\sum_i\mathbb{E}[L_i(z_i)]$ by linearity.
\emph{Cost.} Each $\bm{T}_i^{(c)}$ has one non-zero entry per row, so each step visits every pair
$(h,c)\in\mathcal{H}\times C_i$ once.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:pr}]
\emph{Inner minimisation.} For $\lambda\ge0$, define the Gibbs distribution
$q_\lambda(\bm{z})=p_0(\bm{z})e^{-\lambda\Phi_\mathcal{S}(\bm{z})}/M_\lambda$ with $M_\lambda=\mathbb{E}_{p_0}[e^{-\lambda\Phi_\mathcal{S}}]$.
Rearranging the Lagrangian,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert p_0)+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}_q[\Phi_\mathcal{S}]\\
&\quad=\textstyle\sum_{\bm{z}}q\log\big(q/(p_0e^{-\lambda\Phi_\mathcal{S}})\big)\\
&\quad=\mathrm{KL}(q\Vert q_\lambda)-\log M_\lambda ,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is minimised, uniquely, by $q=q_\lambda$.
\emph{The minimiser is the CRF.} $p_0$ is a product over streams of Eq.~\eqref{eq:crf} at $\lambda=0$, and
$\Phi_\mathcal{S}$ is a sum of per-block delays, so
$p_0(\bm{z})e^{-\lambda\Phi_\mathcal{S}(\bm{z})}=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathds{1}[\bm{z}_{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{U})]\exp\big(\sum_i\phi^0_i-\lambda L_i\big)/Z_0(\mathcal{U})$;
since $\phi^0_i-\lambda L_i=\phi^\lambda_i$, normalising gives $q_\lambda=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}p_\lambda(\cdot\mid\mathcal{U})$ and
$M_\lambda=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}Z_\lambda(\mathcal{U})/Z_0(\mathcal{U})$.
\emph{Dual and its derivatives.} Substituting $q_\lambda$ into the Lagrangian gives
$\mathcal{D}(\lambda)=-\log M_\lambda-\lambda bW_\mathcal{S}$, the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual}. For one stream,
$\partial_\lambda\log Z_\lambda=\sum_{\bm{z}}(-\Phi(\bm{z}))e^{\sum_i\phi^\lambda_i}/Z_\lambda=-\mathbb{E}_{p_\lambda}[\Phi]$, and
differentiating once more, $\partial^2_\lambda\log Z_\lambda=\mathbb{E}_{p_\lambda}[\Phi^2]-\mathbb{E}_{p_\lambda}[\Phi]^2=\mathrm{Var}_{p_\lambda}[\Phi]$.
Streams are independent under $q_\lambda$, so expectations and variances add, which gives the second line.
\emph{Optimality.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:pr} minimises a convex function of $q$ under one linear constraint, and a strictly
feasible point exists, so strong duality holds and $q^\star=q_{\lambda^\star}$ with $\lambda^\star=\arg\max_{\lambda\ge0}\mathcal{D}$.
As $\mathcal{D}''\le0$, $\mathcal{D}'(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}_{p_\lambda}[\Phi_\mathcal{S}]-bW_\mathcal{S}$ is non-increasing: if it
is $\le0$ at $\lambda=0$ the maximum is at $\lambda^\star=0$, otherwise at a root, where the budget holds with equality
(complementary slackness). A non-increasing function has its root found by bisection.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Posterior-weighted training]
\label{prop:patrain}
Let $\pi$ be a distribution over speech-code sequences, $C$ a set of candidates with distinct code sequences,
$\pi(C)=\sum_{c\in C}\pi(c)>0$ and $\tilde{\pi}(c)=\pi(c)/\pi(C)$. For any distribution $\bm{w}\in\Delta^{|C|}$,
$-\sum_{c\in C}w(c)\log\pi(c)=\mathrm{KL}(\bm{w}\Vert\tilde{\bm{\pi}})+\mathrm{H}(\bm{w})-\log\pi(C)$, with $\mathrm{H}$ the
entropy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\log\pi(c)=\log\tilde{\pi}(c)+\log\pi(C)$ and use $\sum_cw(c)=1$:
$-\sum_cw(c)\log\pi(c)=-\sum_cw(c)\log\tilde{\pi}(c)-\log\pi(C)$, and
$-\sum_cw(c)\log\tilde{\pi}(c)=\mathrm{KL}(\bm{w}\Vert\tilde{\bm{\pi}})+\mathrm{H}(\bm{w})$.
Since $\mathrm{H}(\bm{w})$ does not depend on $\pi$, minimising the weighted cross-entropy over $\pi$ moves
$\tilde{\bm{\pi}}$ towards the CRF posterior $\bm{w}$ and raises the mass $\pi(C)$ on renderings of the kind the CRF scored.
The analogy with posterior-regularised EM~\citep{ganchev2010posterior} is that $\bm{w}$ plays the role of the projected
posterior of the E-step; it is not exact, because the potentials also contain the external fidelity score $f$ and the
Talker is fitted at the base-rate backlog $\tilde h_i$ rather than under the marginal over backlogs.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implementation.} Messages are computed in log space with log-sum-exp. $\lambda^\star$ is found by bisection:
$\lambda^\star=0$ if $p_0$ meets the budget; otherwise the interval $[0,50]$ is halved 40 times and the upper end, the
smallest tested price that meets the budget, is returned. On small streams, the forward and backward recursions,
$Z_\lambda$, the marginals and the bisection agree with brute-force enumeration over all candidate sequences (unit tests).

\section{Evaluation Protocols}
\label{app:eval}
\label{app:metrics}
\label{app:prereg}

This section defines cpASR-BLEU and tcpASR-BLEU completely, so that they can be recomputed from transcripts and
reference annotations. tcpBLEU is the same procedure applied to the text output.

\paragraph{Inputs.} For a meeting, the reference consists of annotated utterances $v$ with a speaker $\varrho_v$, a
source span $[a_v,b_v]$ (s) and, if scored, a reference translation $\bm{g}_v$; hesitations and utterances without a
reference are unscored. The output consists of segments $s$ with a speaker label $k_s$ (a slot, or none), a source
span $[a_s,b_s]$ on the meeting clock (from its time tokens; at least 80\,ms long) and a transcript $\bm{o}_s$ (the ASR
transcript of its speech; for tcpBLEU, its text). Concatenation joins with a space in English and without one in
Chinese.

\paragraph{Units and matching.} The scored utterances of each reference speaker, in time order, are chained into units
while the next one starts less than 1\,s after the current unit ends; a unit $u$ has speaker $\varrho_u$, span
$[a_u,b_u]$ (the union) and reference $\bm{g}_u$ (the concatenation). Per meeting, $\bm{o}_k$ concatenates the transcripts
of stream $k$ in time order and $\bm{g}_\varrho$ the scored references of speaker $\varrho$; with
$\mathrm{chrF}(\cdot,\cdot)$ the sentence-level chrF of sacreBLEU (default settings: character 6-grams, $\beta=2$),
\begin{equation}
\sigma^\star=\arg\max_{\sigma}\textstyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{chrF}\big(\bm{o}_k,\bm{g}_{\sigma(k)}\big)
\label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
over one-to-one maps $\sigma$ from labelled, non-empty streams to speakers with non-empty references (Hungarian
algorithm); $\mathcal{K}$ is the set of matched streams, and unlabelled segments are never matched.

\paragraph{Segment assignment.} Let $\mathrm{ov}([a,b],[a',b'])=\min(b,b')-\max(a,a')$. Each segment with a non-empty
transcript is widened by a collar of 2\,s, $[a_s-2,b_s+2]$, and $v^\star$ is the annotated utterance (scored or not) with
the largest positive overlap with it. If there is none, the segment is an insertion. If $v^\star$ is unscored, the
segment is dropped (it lies on speech that is not scored). Otherwise, if $\varrho_{v^\star}=\sigma^\star(k_s)$, the segment
joins the unit of $v^\star$; if not, it joins the unit of speaker $\sigma^\star(k_s)$ with the largest positive overlap
with the widened span, and is an insertion if there is none. A segment whose unwidened span overlaps two or more units of
its matched speaker, each by at least 20\% of the segment's length, is split among them: its words (English) or
characters (Chinese) are divided in time order in proportion to the overlaps.

\paragraph{The two scores.} For tcpASR-BLEU, $\bm{o}_u$ concatenates, in time order, the transcripts (or parts) that joined
unit $u$, and $\mathcal{I}$ is the set of insertions; for cpASR-BLEU, the stream transcripts $\bm{o}_k$ omit the dropped
segments. With $\varnothing$ the empty string,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{tcp}}&=\{(\bm{o}_u,\bm{g}_u)\}_{u}\cup\{(\bm{o}_s,\varnothing)\}_{s\in\mathcal{I}},\\
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{cp}}&=\{(\bm{o}_k,\bm{g}_{\sigma^\star(k)})\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\cup\{(\bm{o}_k,\varnothing)\}_{k\notin\mathcal{K}}\\
&\quad\cup\{(\varnothing,\bm{g}_\varrho)\}_{\varrho\notin\sigma^\star(\mathcal{K})}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pairs}
\end{equation}
For each meeting $m$ and pair set, $\bm{n}_m\in\R^{10}$ collects the BLEU sufficient statistics of sacreBLEU (matching
and total $n$-gram counts for $n=1..4$, hypothesis and reference lengths; tokeniser 13a for English, zh for Chinese).
With several TTS runs, $\bm{n}_m$ is averaged over runs; the score is $\mathrm{BLEU}\big(\sum_m\bm{n}_m\big)$, i.e.\ corpus
BLEU over all meetings. Paired tests resample or sign-flip meetings, using the per-meeting statistics. Duplicate
translations of a unit are kept, not removed.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\small
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
1: for each meeting $m$: chain scored utterances into units (gap $<$1\,s) \\

\paragraph{Example.} Speakers A and B say ``we should start'' and ``yes'' at the same time. A system that speaks
``we should start yes'' in one stream is matched to A; for cpASR-BLEU its pair $(\text{``we should start yes''},
\text{``we should start''})$ contains a spurious word and B's pair $(\varnothing,\text{``yes''})$ a missing one, so the
merged contribution is charged twice. For tcpASR-BLEU, the same ``yes'' spoken in B's stream but placed 5\,s after B's
utterance falls outside the collar and becomes an insertion, so correct content at the wrong time also earns nothing.

\paragraph{Speech latency.} For a reference unit with source duration $|X|$ and $|Y|$ heard target words (words or
Chinese characters) with delays $d_1\le\dots\le d_{|Y|}$ from the unit's start,
$\mathrm{LAAL}=\frac{1}{k^\ast}\sum_{k=1}^{k^\ast}\big(d_k-(k-1)\frac{|X|}{\max(|Y|,|Y^\ast|)}\big)$, with $|Y^\ast|$ the
reference length and $k^\ast$ the first $k$ with $d_k\ge|X|$~\citep{papi2022over}. Block $i$ of a stream starts
playing at $\nu_i+h_i$. LAAL (LAAL$_\text{block}$ in the appendix tables) gives every word of the block
$d_k=\nu_i+h_i$ relative to the unit's start; LAAL$_\text{spread}$ spreads its $W_i$ words evenly over the block's
duration, as Eq.~\eqref{eq:lat}. Speech start is the first heard word of a unit; EndOffset~\citep{ma2020simuleval} runs
from the unit's end to the end of its last heard word. Recognisers: Whisper-large-v3 (English) and Paraformer
(Chinese).

\paragraph{Statistics and analysis plans.} Paired tests are over meetings: exact two-sided sign-flip permutation tests
over all $2^M$ flips and 95\% meeting-bootstrap intervals (10{,}000 resamples). Each study followed an internal analysis
plan written before its test decoding and scoring, with every change recorded as a dated amendment before the results
it touches. The plans fix the meeting lists (AMI test EN2002a--d, ES2004a--d, IS1009a--d, TS3003a--d; all 20 AliMeeting
test meetings; the eight AliMeeting evaluation meetings), the systems, the confirmatory contrasts with their Holm
families (\sys{} $-$ B over twelve BLEU tests; \sys{} $-$ each system of Table~\ref{tab:main} per BLEU score, fixed
before the added systems were decoded) and the development rules for choosing checkpoints.

\section{Baseline Systems}
\label{app:baselines}

\paragraph{Causal relabelling.} SeamlessStreaming, X-Translator and the cascade do not assign their output to meeting
speakers. Each of their segments is assigned to the slot with the highest mean posterior of our diarizer over the
segment's span, using only frames released by its commit time (1.04\,s profile); if none are, over the released frames
from 2\,s before its start. This gives them the speaker attribution of our diarizer, which they lack on their own.
